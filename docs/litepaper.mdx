# Malibu Litepaper

> The world's biggest AI cloud already exists. Malibu turns it on.

## The premise

Roughly two hundred million Apple Silicon Macs have shipped since 2020 (a directional industry estimate — [methodology](/network/benchmarks-and-methodology#market-sizing-claims)). Every one of them has unified memory that can hold open-weight models, and most of them sit idle for much of the day. Fleet idle-time figures are planning assumptions, not audited telemetry — see [Benchmarks & methodology](/network/benchmarks-and-methodology).

Malibu turns Apple Silicon Macs into a coordinated inference network. Admitted providers accrue credits for the verified inference they serve. Token-protocol conversion of buyer dollars into $MALIBU demand is **planned** — see [Economics](/guides/economics). **Malibu doesn't build new AI infrastructure. It activates the infrastructure already sitting on millions of desks.**

## The opportunity

AI inference is a large and fast-growing market, and a handful of companies own most of it. Open-source models — Llama, Qwen, gpt-oss — match closed-model quality on **many chat, coding, and retrieval workloads** (not every reasoning or multimodal eval). Supply hasn't. Buyers often pay a large markup over the underlying compute.

New datacenter capacity takes billions of dollars and years to permit. Meanwhile hundreds of millions of AI-capable Macs sit on desks running at low utilization (the "four percent" figure is a **planning assumption**, not measured fleet telemetry).

Every prior decentralized AI network — Bittensor, Akash, Gensyn, Prime Intellect — hit the same wall: buyers won't come without providers; providers won't stay without buyers. A decade of permanent bootstrap.

**Malibu's bet is to pay providers to be ready before buyers arrive** (PoMW + emission at v0). Today's live product is the **inference marketplace** (credits and receipts) on a small invite-gated pool. USDC-on-Base payout is **planned for public beta** and is off today. Check [Network status](/status) for the ledger.

## Why now

Five years ago this network wasn't possible. Today, four preconditions are simultaneously true for the first time:

- **Apple Silicon.** Unified-memory Macs capable of serving large open-source models are shipping in consumer hardware.
- **Open-source frontier models.** Llama, Qwen, gpt-oss now match closed-model quality on many workloads. Verified inference on open weights is finally worth paying for.
- **Cheap inference verification.** TOPLOC (Prime Intellect, 2025) is designed to verify buyer responses at roughly one percent of serving cost — **planned v1** on Malibu, not live. Receipts are live today.
- **Stablecoin payments.** USDC on Base can make global micropayments settlement-final in seconds, at sub-cent cost. Malibu's USDC payout rail is built but **not live** yet.

**Malibu is not early to any of these. It is the network that combines all four.**

## What we've shipped

Malibu is a **live coordinated inference network** today — distributed Apple Silicon providers, centralized routing and billing. Real coordinator, real providers, real inference requests, real credit ledger. Admission is **private pre-beta**: a valid invite is required to register a new provider. Public API buyers use a free daily token quota and are not charged money in pre-beta. USDC-on-Base payout is planned for public beta.

### Inference marketplace — Live

- **Coordinator service.** `Live` Provider admission, real-time request routing, the credit ledger, and receipt-based settlement.
- **Gateway.** `Live` OpenAI-compatible `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/models` at `api.malibu.tech/v1`, plus `/v1/usage` (daily quota) and `/v1/receipts/{request_id}` (receipt status). Public, no-key `/v1/status`, `/v1/rate-card`, and `/v1/stats/*`. `Beta` Experimental OpenAI Responses (`/v1/responses`) and Anthropic Messages (`/v1/messages`) facades over chat completions.
- **Provider onboarding.** `Beta` The notarized Malibu app installs the provider, benchmarks the Mac, and picks the best paid model that fits. The `malibu-cli` command covers the same flow from a terminal, including headless installs over SSH. **Invite required** for a fresh register ([malibu.tech/host](https://malibu.tech/host)). Already-running providers update without a new invite.
- **Agent-readable onboarding.** `Live` Point a coding agent at [get.malibu.tech/skill.md](https://get.malibu.tech/skill.md) to install, inspect, recover, update, or uninstall the provider CLI, and to connect apps to `api.malibu.tech`. Same invite gate.
- **End-to-end serving.** `Live` Buyer → gateway → coordinator → Apple Silicon provider → response → signed settlement receipt → settlement. Public snapshot: [api.malibu.tech/v1/stats/overview](https://api.malibu.tech/v1/stats/overview).
- **Multi-turn tool calling.** `Live` Standardized `tool_calls[]` emission. Qwen-family models accept `role: "tool"` results on later turns; gpt-oss models support the first turn only. Buyer frameworks **must** validate before execution — [Buyer-side validation](/agentic/buyer-side-validation).
- **Structured output.** `Live` `response_format: json_schema` for a strict schema subset. The provider validates the finished output against your schema; it does not constrain decoding.
- **Sticky conversations.** `Live` Send `X-MacProvider-Conversation` to prefer the provider that served earlier turns. The preference often does not win; when it does, cache hits bill at a lower rate and show in `usage.cached_prompt_tokens`. Buyers cannot pin a request to a specific provider.
- **Signed receipts.** `Live` Most paid requests settle on a signed settlement receipt that the coordinator verifies; the rest carry a legacy signed receipt and settle as quarantined. Some non-streaming responses carry a v0.3 receipt header (currently roughly a quarter to a third); streams never do. For the rest, use `/v1/receipts`. `Pending` Offline verification of current receipts is pending a verifier update.
- **Verified model settlement.** `Live` Missing, invalid, or late receipts settle with zero provider credit and no buyer debit.
- **Rate card & credits.** `Live` Credit-based metering at a **90% provider share today**. Payout wallet registration is live; USDC-on-Base payout is **planned for public beta**. See [Economics](/guides/economics).
- **Model catalog.** `Live` A signed catalog of 17 open-weight MLX models — Llama 3.2 3B and 3.1 8B, Qwen3 / Qwen3.5 / Qwen3.6 / Qwen3.8 and Qwen-Coder rows, Gemma 4, Nemotron 3 Nano, GLM-4.5-Air, gpt-oss-20b and gpt-oss-120b. A model routes only when an eligible Mac with enough memory is online and serving it. Live list: [api.malibu.tech/v1/status](https://api.malibu.tech/v1/status). Details: [Model catalog](/operations/model-catalog).
- **Routing tiers.** `Live` New Macs join **provisional**; providers that meet the network's trust criteria move to **trusted** and full routing weight. See [Payments → Routing tiers](/guides/payments#routing-tiers).
- **Hardware attestation.** `Beta` Apple-backed device attestation is reported for a small part of the pool. It does not affect routing or settlement yet.
- **Release cadence.** `Live` Signed provider releases on GitHub with signed autoupdate; public download follows GitHub Latest via [malibu.tech/host](https://malibu.tech/host).

### Token protocol — Planned

- **PoMW mining substrate.** `Prototype` A Metal port of the Proof-of-Model-Walk algorithm passed two hundred verification vectors byte-exact against a Rust reference on Qwen3-1.7B GGUF weights. Internal Phase 1 measurements on an M5 Air (July 2026) — about 2.14 million walks per second at about 4.7 watts, and roughly ten times the per-watt walk rate of an RTX 5090 — are **unaudited prototype numbers**: the results report is not yet published, and they measure the walk workload, not inference. See [Benchmarks & methodology](/network/benchmarks-and-methodology#pomw-phase-1-benchmark-m5-air).
- **v0 mining launch:** `Planned v0` on-chain emission, burn-and-mint fee split, token-native settlement.
- **v1 marketplace:** `Planned v1` TOPLOC per-request verification (Prime Intellect lineage) on buyer-paid inference.
- **Pooled serving:** `Planned` multi-Mac models beyond one machine's unified memory.

**This is a launch document for a network that already runs inference — and a protocol layer still activating.**

## What Malibu lets you do

Your Mac already has the hardware open models need: unified memory, a fast GPU, and low power draw. Malibu gives that idle capacity a job. The Malibu app installs a provider that serves AI requests to buyers in the background, and you can pause it from the menu bar at any time.

A single high-RAM Mac can serve large open models from unified memory. The models that dominate industry inference spend are larger still; **pooled serving** (several Macs over a local interconnect) is the growth vector for those — **on the roadmap, not live**. And the bigger the model, the more a verifiable receipt is worth — when you pay frontier rates, you want evidence that the endpoint ran the weights you paid for, not a cheaper stand-in.

What a provider earns today is simple to state and impossible to promise: **90% of the credits** for the verified inference its Mac serves, at the rates on the public rate card. How much traffic a Mac gets depends on buyer demand, uptime, routing tier, which models it can fit, and thermal limits. Credits accrue on the ledger now; USDC payout is planned for public beta. Malibu publishes no per-Mac earnings figure. The mechanics and the hardware caveats are on [Provider economics](/guides/provider-economics).

Cooperative scheduling keeps the Mac yours. Mining (planned) is designed to run in the background and yield to serving.

## What buyers get

On comparable OSS SKUs, Malibu is often cheaper than aggregator list — **sometimes a lot cheaper on small models, and on some 20B–32B rows even or higher (up to about 59% higher on gpt-oss-20b in the dated snapshot)**. Dated snapshot: [Pricing comparison](/guides/pricing-comparison). Live card: [api.malibu.tech/v1/rate-card](https://api.malibu.tech/v1/rate-card). Paid requests settle on signed receipts checked by the coordinator. Offline buyer verification of current receipts is pending a verifier update.

The signed catalog covers 17 open-weight models, from Llama 3.2 3B up to Qwen3.x, Qwen-Coder, Gemma 4, Nemotron 3 Nano, GLM-4.5-Air and gpt-oss-120b — **each runs only when a Mac with enough memory is online and serving it** ([Model catalog](/operations/model-catalog)). Drop-in OpenAI-compatible endpoints; in pre-beta, public API use is covered by a free daily token quota.

Why buyers switch:

- **Cheaper on many OSS rows.** Verify against the snapshot; do not assume a flat 20–40% on every SKU.
- **Receipt-verified.** Signed binding of prompt and output hashes to the provider's key and its reported model hash — see [Signed receipts](/guides/receipts) for what that does and does not prove. Per-request forward-pass attestation via [TOPLOC](/network/toploc) ships at v1 — not live on buyer traffic today.
- **OSS-native.** Best model per task, when that model is warm. No vendor deprecating a closed SKU next quarter.
- **Cooperative trust.** Not confidential compute — prompts route through providers as plaintext. See [Security & trust model](/network/security) and [Threat model](/network/threat-model).
- **Buyer-side validation.** Emitted `tool_calls[]` are model output, not provider-verified intent. See [Buyer-side validation](/agentic/buyer-side-validation).
- **No lock-in.** Change providers without changing OpenAI-SDK code.

**Malibu is designed to be adopted without changing anything on the buyer side except the endpoint.**

## How it works

Every provider runs one binary. At mining launch it performs **Proof-of-Model-Walk** — a byte-exact 256-step walk through the canonical open-source model's weights, terminated by a cryptographic commitment. Deterministic. Verifiable in microseconds.

The walk hashes over the same weight file the network sells inference for, using the same Metal integer pipeline. Every mined block is a receipt that a specific Mac exercised the memory substrate inference depends on. When a buyer request arrives at v1, the miner yields in ~50 milliseconds — weights already resident, pipeline already running, file already the file the buyer is paying for.

**Mining state is serving state.** That equivalence is the architectural bet the whole network is built on. It is **not** enforced in production today.

Verification is cheap. PoMW verifies in microseconds. TOPLOC (Prime Intellect, 2025) will verify buyer responses at about one percent of serving cost at v1.

## The token flywheel

Under the planned token economics, every dollar entering Malibu would strengthen the network: providers would get paid, reserves would grow, and tokens would be bought and burned automatically. None of this is live today.

<Note>
  **Today (pre-beta marketplace):** providers earn **90%** of gross credits; operator retains 10%. Credits accrue on the ledger. USDC-on-Base payout is planned for public beta. See [Economics](/guides/economics).

  **At token launch (planned):** the flywheel below replaces the marketplace split.
</Note>

When a buyer pays $1 for inference under token economics:

- **70¢** goes to the provider who served the request.
- **12¢** grows a protocol-owned reserves floor — held in USDC, on-chain, backing the token's minimum value.
- **18¢** automatically buys $MALIBU on the open market and destroys it.

No oracle. No governance vote. No token-holder decision. Every transaction is protocol-enforced $MALIBU demand and protocol-owned reserve growth. **The token, minted through work, is burned through use.**

Provider issuance runs on a deterministic emission curve — front-loaded to bootstrap the fleet, tapering as buyer USDC replaces it.

## Why Malibu wins

1. **Apple already shipped the hardware.** Roughly two hundred million Apple Silicon Macs with unified memory. Deployed, paid for, and often idle.
2. **PoMW is designed to prepare the fleet before buyers arrive.** Providers earn from day one of mining launch for verifiable useful work. That layer is **not** paying in production yet.
3. **Receipts today; TOPLOC at v1.** Every paid request settles on a signed receipt now. TOPLOC adds per-request forward-pass attestation when that milestone ships.
4. **Every transaction is designed to strengthen the token.** At launch, buyer USDC automatically buys and burns $MALIBU; reserves grow. Not active on today's 90/10 credit rail.
5. **The network compounds.** Every new provider increases coverage. Better coverage attracts more buyers. Buyer revenue increases provider earnings. Higher earnings attract more providers.
6. **The combination is the bet — not a monopoly on the pieces.** Akash, Prime Intellect, Render, and io.net already cover overlapping slices (decentralized compute, verification lineage, GPU marketplaces). Malibu's edge is Apple Silicon + receipts now + PoMW-before-demand as a token mechanism those businesses have little reason to copy. **The hardware is not Malibu's. The mechanism has to come from outside Apple, OpenAI, and the hyperscalers.**
